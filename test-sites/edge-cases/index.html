Edge cases

This page is deliberately awkward. It contains unusual identifiers, duplicate ids, transformed text, horizontal scrolling, SVG text, unselectable text, text that is replaced by script, and a long tail of filler so that the page is well over four thousand pixels tall.

Odd identifiers

This paragraph's id contains a double quotation mark, which breaks naive XPath strings of the form //*[@id="..."], because the quotes no longer balance.

This paragraph's id contains an apostrophe. In an XPath written with single quotes that would be the problem; in one written with double quotes it is fine, which is rather the point.

First paragraph with the duplicate id. An XPath of //*[@id="dupe"] returns whichever the browser finds first, so a selection in the second one may be re-anchored into this one.

Second paragraph with the very same id. Text in here should be highlighted in here, not in its twin above.

Transformed text

The long tail

Surveyors of the period tended to describe landscape in terms of what was wrong with it. A hill was never simply a hill; it was a hill that would be difficult to drain. A river was a river that had opinions about property boundaries. It makes their reports a pleasure to read, if not to act on.

Modern readers sometimes assume that the past was quieter. The archive suggests otherwise. There are petitions about church bells, complaints about geese, and a long, bitter dispute over who had the right to cut the reeds on a pond that has since been drained and built over by a petrol station.

What survives is a kind of accidental portrait: a community defined less by its great events than by its small, persistent irritations. If you want to know what a place was like, do not ask what happened there. Ask what people wrote to the council about.

The county archive keeps, in a cellar that smells faintly of apples, a box of letters that nobody has ever catalogued. They are mostly about drains. One correspondent, writing in 1894, complains for eleven pages about a culvert, and the reply, which is also in the box, is a single line thanking him for his interest.
